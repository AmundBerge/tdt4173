We interpreted the final random forest configuration using impurity-based feature importance, averaged equally across the 14 generating units. Vinjevatn's standardized hourly price-minus-water-value margin had the highest importance (16.62%), followed by its weekly mean margin (5.08%) and Vaamarvatn's hourly margin (4.92%). This suggests that the forests use both the current price relative to end-of-week water value and profitability over surrounding forecast hours when predicting commitment. These importances summarize reductions in training-set impurity; correlated features can share importance, and the values establish neither effect direction nor causality.
